How to Use NorseLift with Grok, Grok Bot and in Your Tesla

Add NorseLift as a custom MCP connector in Grok, use it from Grok Bot, and what Tesla says about Grok connectors in the car.

By NorseLiftOctober 15, 2026Updated October 20, 20265 min read

Background photo: Bram Van Oost on Unsplash, tinted and overlaid by NorseLift.

Grok can talk to NorseLift. Connect it once and you can ask about your training, log a session or plan your week, whether you're on grok.com, in Grok Bot, or hands-free in your Tesla.

Picture this

It's Wednesday morning and you're driving to the gym.

Hey Grok, what's my workout today?
Today is Posterior Upper in your Aesthetic 3-Day routine: barbell rows, lat pulldowns, seated cable rows, face pulls and tricep pushdowns. Last time you rowed 3 × 10 at 70 kg, so try 72.5 kg today.
Illustrative example using the demo routine "Aesthetic 3-Day". Grok reads your NorseLift routine and history through the connector; exact wording will vary.

You need NorseLift Pro (paid, gifted or referral) for Connect your AI, and the NorseLift MCP server URL: https://app.norselift.com/mcp. New to MCP? Start with how to connect your AI to NorseLift.

Option 1: Add NorseLift as a custom connector on grok.com

Grok supports custom MCP servers as connectors. According to xAI's Grok documentation, connectors are available to all Grok users, and you add a custom MCP server like this:

  1. Go to grok.com/connectors.
  2. Click New Connector, then Custom.
  3. Paste https://app.norselift.com/mcp and complete the sign-in. NorseLift opens its own page where you approve the connection.
  4. Start a new chat and ask something NorseLift can answer, like "What did I train this week?"

Grok discovers the tools the server exposes and uses them when your question needs them. On Grok Business and Enterprise plans, an admin has to add connectors for the team first.

Option 2: Use NorseLift from Grok Bot

Grok Bot is xAI's assistant app that runs multi-step tasks for you. It can use MCP connectors too: add NorseLift as a custom connector (in NorseLift's AI assistants settings the steps are simply "Add a custom connector" and "Paste the URL"), sign in to NorseLift when asked, and then use it in a chat. Third-party guides describe asking Grok Bot in a chat to add the server, approving it, and attaching it with @. Check your Grok plan: some guides note that certain connector and Grok Bot features need a paid subscription.

Grok Bot is handy for multi-step requests: "Look at my last four weeks, then build me a 3-day routine that brings up my back, schedule it Mon/Wed/Fri and make it active."

Option 3: Grok in your Tesla

Grok is available in supported Tesla vehicles. Tesla's support page says:

  • Connectors need vehicle software 2026.26 or later and a signed-in Grok account in the car (plus an AMD processor and Premium Connectivity or Wi-Fi for Grok itself).
  • Connectors are set up from your phone or computer, not the touchscreen. Sign the car in with the same Grok account you use on your phone, and Grok uses your connectors automatically.
  • Grok Bot in the car needs a SuperGrok subscription.

So the setup is: add NorseLift as a custom connector on grok.com (Option 1), then sign in to Grok in the car with the same account. Then ask by voice: "What's my workout today?" or "Log today's leg session: squats 4×6 at 100 kg."

Good to know: Tesla's own examples use built-in connectors like Gmail and Google Calendar, but custom connectors like NorseLift work in the car too: Grok can read your training, log and edit workouts, and build, schedule and activate routines, just like on grok.com. The vehicle and account requirements above still apply. And please keep it hands-free: don't log sets while driving.
